The problem it solves

Settings travel without context: the 300 mm/min that worked in the video came from a different wattage, a different plywood batch, different air assist. Meanwhile the joint that should press together rattles, because nobody subtracted the kerf - the beam eats a sliver from every edge, and that sliver is exact geometry, not bad luck.

Laser math gives you a labeled starting point for your material, thickness and wattage; the exact kerf compensation for box joints, inlays and press fits; and a diagnosis for the four failures every laser owner meets: the cut that didn't go through, the charred edge, the flame, and the wavy line. Settings are illustrative starting points from published community tables - fires are real, vinyl and PVC are never cut, and the machine is never left alone.

Kerf compensation is exact; the speed model is labeled guidance your scrap bin should verify.
